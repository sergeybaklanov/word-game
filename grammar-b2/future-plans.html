Decision tree — which future form to use
All 5 forms — summary table
FormWhen to useExample
will + VSpontaneous decision; prediction (opinion); promise; offer"The phone rang." → "I'll get it!"
be going to + VPre-planned intention; prediction from visible evidence"I'm going to apply for that job." / "Look — it's going to snow."
Present ContinuousFixed arrangement — booked, confirmed, in the diary"We're meeting them at 7 on Friday." (table booked)
Present SimpleOfficial timetable / schedule (transport, programmes)"The conference starts at 9 am on Monday."
will be + V-ingAction in progress at a specific future moment; polite enquiry"This time tomorrow I'll be flying to Rome."
Other structures for future meaning
be about to
Immediate future — about to happen any second
"The film is about to start — hurry!"
be due to
Scheduled / expected at a particular time
"The train is due to arrive at 14:35."
be likely to
Probably will happen (prediction with probability)
"Prices are likely to rise next year."
be set to
Formal — officially planned / expected to happen
"She is set to become the next director."
ℹ
Future in the past When reporting or narrating past events, use was/were going to, would, or was about to to describe what was future from that past perspective: "She said she was going to resign." / "I was about to leave when he called."
