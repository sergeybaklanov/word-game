B2 Special Topic
Passive Voice
Пасивний стан — focus on the action or the object, not who does it
What is the passive and when to use it?

In the active voice, the subject performs the action. In the passive, the subject receives the action. You choose passive when:

→ The agent (doer) is unknown, unimportant, or obvious: "My wallet was stolen." (we don't know by whom)

→ You want to focus on the result/object, not on who did it: "The bridge was built in 1899."

→ In formal, scientific, or news writing to sound objective: "The experiment was conducted…"

→ To avoid saying who is responsible (diplomatic language): "A mistake was made."

Formation: be (correct tense) + V3
Present Simple
am/is/are + V3
English is spoken here.
Present Continuous
am/is/are being + V3
The road is being repaired.
Present Perfect
have/has been + V3
The letter has been sent.
Past Simple
was/were + V3
The bridge was built in 1899.
Past Continuous
was/were being + V3
The report was being written.
Past Perfect
had been + V3
The work had been completed.
Future (will)
will be + V3
It will be delivered tomorrow.
Modals
modal + be + V3
It should be done now.
Active → Passive transformation
ActivePassive
They build houses every year.Houses are built every year.
Someone stole my wallet.My wallet was stolen.
They have cancelled the flight.The flight has been cancelled.
A technician will fix it.It will be fixed (by a technician).
Passive with "by" and "with"

Use by to mention the agent (who performed the action): "The novel was written by Tolstoy." — Only include by + agent when it adds important information.

Use with to mention the instrument or tool: "The window was broken with a hammer." — with introduces the thing used, not the person.

Passive with two objects (give, send, show, offer…)

Some verbs take two objects. Either can become the subject of the passive. The indirect object (person) is more commonly made the subject in English:

ActivePassive (object as subject)Passive (person as subject)
They gave me a prize.A prize was given to me.I was given a prize. (more natural)
She showed him the results.The results were shown to him.He was shown the results.
⚠
Not all verbs can be passive. Intransitive verbs (verbs with no object — arrive, sleep, happen, exist) cannot be used in the passive: ✗ "It was happened yesterday." ✓ "It happened yesterday."
