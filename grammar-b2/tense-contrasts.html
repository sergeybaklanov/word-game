B2 Special Topic
Key Tense Contrasts
The most commonly confused tense pairs at B2 level
1. Present Perfect vs Past Simple

The most common error at B2. The key principle: Past Simple needs a specific past time (yesterday, last week, in 2019, at 3 pm). Present Perfect has no specific time — or the time is unfinished (this week, today, recently). In American English, Past Simple is often used where British English uses Present Perfect — but for B2, follow British rules.

Present Perfect — no specific time
I have seen that film. (unspecified, ever)
She has just left. (very recent)
Have you ever been to Paris?
Past Simple — specific time given
I saw that film last week.
She left at 3 pm.
Did you go to Paris in 2018?
2. Past Simple vs Past Continuous
Past Simple — completed, short event
The phone rang. (short completed)
I turned off the light.
Past Continuous — background, in progress
I was sleeping when the phone rang.
She was watching TV at midnight.
3. Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous
PP Simple — completed / result
I have read that book. (finished)
She has made a cake. (it's ready)
PP Continuous — duration / activity
I have been reading for hours. (still going or just stopped)
She has been making cakes all day. (effort/time)
4. 4 ways to express the future
FormWhen to useExample
will + VSpontaneous decision, opinion-based prediction, promise"I'll get it!" / "I think it'll rain."
be going to + VPre-planned intention, evidence-based prediction"I'm going to study medicine." / "Look — it's going to rain."
Present ContinuousFixed arrangement (booked, confirmed, in the diary)"We're flying to Rome on Saturday." (tickets booked)
Present SimpleScheduled / timetabled (transport, official events)"The train leaves at 10:15."
B2 Special Topic
Time Clauses
Підрядні речення часу — the tense rules after when, as soon as, until, before, after…
The golden rule
⚠
After time conjunctions, NEVER use will or would. Use a present or past tense instead.
✗ "I'll call you when I will arrive." ✓ "I'll call you when I arrive."
✗ "We'll leave as soon as she will finish." ✓ "We'll leave as soon as she finishes."
Time conjunctions and their tense patterns
ConjunctionTense in time clauseExample
whenPresent Simple (for future)I'll tell her when I see her.
as soon asPresent Simple (for future)Call me as soon as you arrive.
beforePresent Simple or Present PerfectFinish the task before you go home.
afterPresent Perfect (to show completion)After you have finished, send it to me.
until / tillPresent Simple (for future)Wait here until I come back.
oncePresent Simple or Present PerfectOnce she arrives, we'll start.
by the timePresent Simple → Future Perfect in mainBy the time you read this, I'll have left.
Present Perfect in time clauses — showing sequence

When you want to show that one action must be completely finished before the next begins, use Present Perfect in the time clause. This is more precise than Present Simple:

"I'll send the report once I've checked it." (checking must be fully done first)

"Let me know when you've read the document." (reading must be complete)

Compare: "Call me when you finish." (more casual) vs "Call me when you have finished." (emphasis on completion)
