Answers with timeline explanations
gap 1
"By the time my line manager _____ (introduce) me to everyone… it was time for lunch."
had introduced Past Perfect
Rule: The introduction was completed before another past moment (lunchtime). "By the time" always signals Past Perfect — it means one action was fully done before a second past action.
gap 2
"I _____ (never see) an office canteen before and was amazed."
had never seen Past Perfect
Rule: Life experience up to a past moment. "Before" triggers Past Perfect — she had zero canteen experience before that specific lunch visit in the past.
gap 3
"I _____ (work) on the newsletter for a while when my manager invited me…"
was working Past Continuous
Rule: Longer background action (working on newsletter) was interrupted by a short Past Simple event (manager invited her). Classic pattern: was V-ing + when + Past Simple.
gap 4
"I _____ (prepare) for weeks for the interview so I was pleased it went smoothly."
had been preparing Past Perfect Continuous
Rule: Ongoing preparation over a period ("for weeks") that ended at a specific past moment (the interview). Duration + completion before another past event = Past Perfect Continuous.
gap 5
"I _____ (forget) how demanding people can be."
had forgotten Past Perfect
Rule: The forgetting happened earlier — before the moment of realisation (getting the flood of emails). Past Perfect captures what was already true at that past reference point.
gap 6
"I _____ (spend) an hour or so looking through them when a really nice one dropped in."
was spending Past Continuous
Rule: Same pattern as gap 3. An ongoing background action (spending an hour going through emails) is interrupted by a sudden short event (a nice email dropped in).
gap 7
"I _____ (already reduce) my working hours to three days…"
have already reduced Present Perfect
Rule: A past action with a clear present result. "Already" signals recency. The hours are currently 3 days — the reduction happened in the past but is still relevant right now.
gap 8
"I _____ (look forward to) that for ages."
have been looking forward to Present Perfect Continuous
Rule: An ongoing feeling that started in the past and is still true now. "For ages" signals a long duration. Present Perfect Continuous = emphasis on how long the feeling has been going on up to this moment.
gap 9
"I _____ (always plan) to set up my own business when I retired, but… I've decided against it!"
had always planned Past Perfect
Rule: The plan existed for a long time in the past — it was an old, long-standing intention before the retirement moment. Past Perfect captures this "background" past state that was then overturned by a newer decision (Present Perfect: "I've decided").
