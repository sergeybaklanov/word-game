B2 Topic

Infinitives

One tiny word, "to", combines with six different verb patterns to show time and voice — same moment or earlier, active or passive, a single action or one still in progress.

B2 Special Topic
The six forms
Шість форм інфінітива — одна й та сама частка «to», різний час і стан дії

The infinitive isn't just "to + verb". By changing what comes after to, you can place the action at the same time as the main verb, earlier than it, in progress, or on the receiving end of it (passive) — all without a separate clause.

1 · Simple infinitive — to + V1
PatternExampleПереклад
to + V1She wants to learn Italian.Вона хоче вивчити італійську.
to + V1He seems to like his new job.Здається, йому подобається нова робота.
to + V1To succeed, you need patience.Щоб досягти успіху, потрібне терпіння.
ℹ
Basic infinitive Same time as the main verb, a future/general reference, or purpose ("to succeed" = in order to succeed). This is the default, neutral form.
2 · Simple passive infinitive — to be + V3
PatternExampleПереклад
to be + V3The report needs to be finished by Friday.Звіт треба закінчити до п'ятниці.
to be + V3She was the first person to be offered the job.Вона була першою людиною, якій запропонували цю роботу.
ℹ
Same time, passive voice The subject doesn't perform the action — it receives it. Same time reference as the simple infinitive, just passive.
3 · Continuous infinitive — to be + V-ing
PatternExampleПереклад
to be + V-ingHe seems to be avoiding me.Здається, він мене уникає.
to be + V-ingShe pretended to be sleeping when I walked in.Вона вдала, що спить, коли я зайшов.
ℹ
In progress, right now The action is happening at the same time as the main verb — it's ongoing, not a finished fact.
4 · Perfect infinitive — to have + V3
PatternExampleПереклад
to have + V3He claims to have met the president.Він стверджує, що зустрічався з президентом.
to have + V3She seems to have forgotten our meeting.Здається, вона забула про нашу зустріч.
not to have + V3I'm sorry not to have replied sooner.Шкода, що я не відповів раніше.
ℹ
Earlier, and finished The action happened before the main verb and is complete — the same relationship Past Perfect has to Past Simple, but packed into an infinitive.
5 · Perfect passive infinitive — to have been + V3
PatternExampleПереклад
to have been + V3The painting is believed to have been stolen in the 1990s.Вважають, що картину викрали в 1990-х.
to have been + V3He claims to have been offered the job twice.Він стверджує, що йому двічі пропонували цю роботу.
ℹ
Earlier, and passive Combine rule 4 (earlier, finished) with rule 2 (passive) — the subject received an action that happened before the main verb.
6 · Perfect continuous infinitive — to have been + V-ing
PatternExampleПереклад
to have been + V-ingShe seems to have been crying.Схоже, вона плакала.
to have been + V-ingHe appeared to have been waiting for hours.Здавалося, він чекав уже кілька годин.
ℹ
Started earlier, over a period The action began before the main verb and either went on for a while or left a visible result right up to that moment — her red eyes, his tired face.
B2 Special Topic
Choosing the right form
Одне дієслово-вступник, різні форми інфінітива — залежно від того, коли і як відбулася дія
Same verb, different time
FormExampleПереклад
simpleHe seems to like it.Здається, йому подобається (зараз, стан).
continuousHe seems to be lying.Здається, він бреше (саме зараз, у процесі).
perfectHe seems to have left.Здається, він уже пішов (раніше, завершено).
perfect continuousHe seems to have been crying.Схоже, він плакав (тривало раніше, помітний результат).
✓
Verbs that commonly take a perfect infinitive seem, appear, claim, pretend, happen, turn out, and the passive reporting verbs be said/believed/reported/thought/known to — all of these can push the action into the past relative to themselves, exactly like Past Perfect does for a narrative.
Active vs passive — who does it, who receives it
ActivePassive
to finish (the report)to be finished
to have met (someone)to have been met
to have been chasing (someone)to have been chased
⚠
Common mistake The perfect infinitive always uses V3, never V2: ✗ "He seems to have went." ✓ "He seems to have gone." — see Past Tenses for the full list of irregular V2/V3 forms.
✓
The pattern in one line simple = same time · +be = passive · +have = earlier and finished · +have been = earlier and passive, or earlier and ongoing (with -ing). Mix the two building blocks (be for passive, have for earlier) and you get all six forms.
