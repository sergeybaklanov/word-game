Zero conditional — general truths

Used for facts that are always true: scientific laws, cause-and-effect rules, habitual results. Both clauses use Present Simple. You can often replace if with when without changing the meaning.

+
If/When + Present Simple,   Present Simple
✎
"If you heat water to 100°C, it boils." | "When I'm tired, I make mistakes."
First conditional — real / likely future

Used for situations that are genuinely possible. The speaker believes the condition could realistically happen. Variations: unless (= if not), as long as, provided that, in case can replace if.

+
If + Present Simple,   will + V
✎
"If it rains, I'll stay home." | "Unless you hurry, you'll miss the train."
⚠
NEVER "will" in the if-clause: ✗ "If it will rain…" ✓ "If it rains…"
Second conditional — unreal present / unlikely future

Used for imaginary or hypothetical situations — things that are not true now, or are very unlikely. Also used for giving advice politely: "If I were you, I would…" — Note: use were for all persons in formal/written English, though was is common informally.

+
If + Past Simple (were for all persons),   would + V
✎
"If I had a car, I would drive to work." | "If I were you, I wouldn't do that."
Third conditional — unreal past (regret / criticism)

Used to talk about past situations that did not happen — imagining a different past outcome. Common for expressing regret ("I wish I had…") or criticising ("You should have…"). The situation is 100% impossible now because the past is over.

+
If + Past Perfect,   would have + V3
✎
"If I had studied more, I would have passed." | "If she had called, I would have helped."
Mixed conditional — B2 key structure

The most important B2 conditional. The if-clause is about a past event that didn't happen; the main clause shows how the present would be different as a result. Look for "now", "today", "still", "at the moment" in the result clause — these signal that the consequence is in the present.

+
If + Past Perfect (unreal past),   would + V now (present result)
✎
"If I hadn't missed that train, I would be in Paris now."
✎
"If she had taken the job, she wouldn't be working here today."
3rd conditional (past→past)
If I had studied, I would have passed the exam. (past result)
Mixed conditional (past→present)
If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now. (present result)
