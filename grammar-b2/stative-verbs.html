What are stative verbs?

Stative verbs describe a state (a condition that exists) rather than an action (something actively happening). States are not "in progress" — they simply exist. This is why stative verbs are not used in the -ing form. Compare: "I eat lunch" (action — you can see it happening) vs "I know the answer" (state — it's just true, there's no activity to observe).

The key test: Can you physically watch someone doing this? If yes → action verb. If no → likely stative.

Dual-meaning verbs (*) — B2 key skill

Some verbs can be either stative or dynamic depending on meaning. When they describe a state, no continuous. When they describe an action, continuous is possible.

Stative meaning (no -ing)Dynamic meaning (can use -ing)
I think it's a good idea. (= I believe/opinion)I am thinking about moving abroad. (= mental activity)
This coffee tastes bitter. (= a property it has)The chef is tasting the soup. (= performing the action)
She has a new car. (= possession)She is having lunch. (= eating — an activity)
He is rude. (= permanent character)He is being rude right now. (= temporary behaviour)
It looks great. (= appears to be)She is looking at me. (= directing eyes)
The bag weighs 5 kg. (= has a weight)They are weighing the parcel. (= measuring it)
