Wishes & Regrets

wish, if only, it's time, would rather — expressing wishes, regrets and preferences at B2.

B2 Special Topic
Wishes & Regrets
Бажання та жалкування — wish, if only, it's time, would rather
wish + Past Simple — wish about the present

Use wish + Past Simple to express a desire for the present situation to be different. The situation is NOT true right now. Use were (not was) for all persons in formal English, though was is acceptable informally.

+
I wish I knew the answer. (= I don't know it now)
✎
"I wish I were taller." / "She wishes she had more time."
wish + Past Perfect — regret about the past

Use wish + Past Perfect to express regret about something that happened (or didn't happen) in the past. The situation cannot be changed. This is structurally identical to the third conditional.

+
I wish I had studied harder. (= I didn't, and I regret it)
✎
"She wishes she hadn't said that." / "They wish they had arrived earlier."
wish + would — wish about someone's behaviour

Use wish + would to express annoyance or desire for someone else to change their behaviour. This is often slightly critical in tone. You cannot use wish + I would (use wish + Past Simple for yourself).

+
I wish you would stop interrupting me!
✎
"She wishes he would call more often." / "I wish it would stop raining."
✎
✗ "I wish I would be taller." → ✓ "I wish I were taller."
if only — stronger emotion

If only follows the same tense rules as wish but expresses stronger emotion — deep regret, strong desire, or frustration. It's more dramatic and emphatic.

If only I knew the answer!Якби тільки я знав відповідь!Past Simple (present wish)

It's time / It's high time

These expressions use Past Simple (even though referring to the present or future) to indicate that something should have happened already or should happen very soon. It's high time is more urgent and critical.

+
It's time you went to bed. (= you should go now)
✎
"It's high time they fixed this road." / "It's time we had a serious conversation."
✎
Also: "It's time to + V" (infinitive) = less urgent: "It's time to go."
would rather — preference

Would rather expresses preference. When the subject of both verbs is the same, use infinitive without to. When talking about what you want someone else to do, use Past Simple after rather.

+
I would rather stay home than go out. (same subject — infinitive)
+
I would rather you stayed home. (different subject — Past Simple)
