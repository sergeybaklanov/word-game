B2 Topic

Past Habits & Plans

used to, would, was going to, was supposed to — three ways to talk about past habits and unfulfilled intentions.

B2 Special Topic
Used to / Would / Past Simple
Three ways to talk about past habits — and the key differences
used to + V

Used to describes past habits or states that no longer exist. It always implies contrast with the present — the situation has changed. It can be used with both action verbs and stative verbs.

+
I used to smoke. (but I don't now)
–
I didn't use to like coffee. (but I do now)
?
Did you use to play football?
✎
Note: "use to" (no -d) in negatives and questions
EnglishУкраїнська
She used to live in Lviv. (she doesn't now)Колись вона жила у Львові.
I didn't use to like jazz. (but I do now)Раніше я не любив джаз.
They used to be good friends. (stative — state, not action)Колись вони були добрими друзями.
would + V (for repeated past actions only)

Would is used for repeated past actions only — things you did regularly in the past. It cannot replace used to with stative verbs (know, be, live, have, believe…) because states cannot be "repeated actions".

+
Every summer, we would go to the seaside.
✎
✗ "I would know him well." (stative) ✓ "I used to know him well."
✎
✗ "She would be shy." (stative) ✓ "She used to be shy."
EnglishCorrect?Why
Every Sunday, he would cook breakfast for everyone.✓repeated action
We would go to the park after school.✓repeated action

Past Simple — neutral past

Past Simple can describe past habits too, but it carries no implication that the situation has changed. It's simply neutral — it just says what happened. Use Past Simple when you give a specific time, or when the contrast with the present isn't the point.

used to — implies change
She used to work at a school. (implies: she doesn't now)
Past Simple — neutral
She worked at a school for ten years. (neutral, no implication)
B2 Special Topic
Past Plans & Intentions
Минулі плани — was going to, was supposed to, was meant to, would
Overview — talking about unfulfilled past plans

These structures describe things you planned, intended, or were expected to do in the past — but often didn't (or did). The key contrast with Past Simple is that they emphasise the intention, not just the action.

was / were going to

Used for a plan or intention that existed in the past — often one that didn't happen due to a change of circumstances. This is the past form of "be going to".

+
Subject + was/were going to + V
✎
I was going to call you, but I forgot. (intended to, didn't do it)
✎
We were going to travel to Italy, but the flights were cancelled.
Timeline — plan existed, didn't happen
was / were supposed to

Used when someone was expected or obligated to do something — by arrangement, rule, or agreement — but usually didn't. Implies mild criticism or disappointment.

+
Subject + was/were supposed to + V
✎
He was supposed to call at 3 pm. (he didn't — implied disappointment)
✎
You weren't supposed to tell her! (you broke the agreement)
was / were meant to

Very similar to was supposed to but slightly more informal. Used for plans, arrangements, or things intended by design. Also used to say something was predestined.

+
Subject + was/were meant to + V
✎
The report was meant to be finished by Friday. (it wasn't)
✎
We were meant to meet at 8 — where were you?
would — habitual past actions (narrative)

Would describes things that regularly happened in the past — typical behaviour, routines, or repeated actions. Used in nostalgic or narrative contexts. Cannot be used with stative verbs (use used to instead).

+
Subject + would + V (repeated past action)
✎
When I was a child, we would spend summers at the lake.
✎
Every Friday he would bring flowers home. (habitual, nostalgic)
Contrast: all four structures
StructureMeaning / nuanceExample
was going toPersonal intention — changed / didn't happenI was going to study abroad, but I changed my mind.
was supposed toExternal expectation / arrangement — often brokenShe was supposed to arrive at 9. (she didn't)
was meant toIntended purpose or plan — slightly informalThis was meant to be a surprise. (it wasn't!)
