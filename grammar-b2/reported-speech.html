Reported Speech
Непряма мова — tenses shift back one step when reporting past statements
Why does tense backshift happen?

When you report what someone said in the past, you move the tenses one step back in time — this is called backshift. It happens because the reporting verb (said, told, asked…) is past, so the reported information also shifts into a more distant past. Think of it as a "time-travel effect".

Direct: "I am tired." → Reported: She said she was tired. (present → past)

Tense backshift table
Direct speech (what was said)→ Reported speech (backshifted)
Present Simple: "I work here."Past Simple: He said he worked there.
Present Continuous: "I am working."Past Continuous: She said she was working.
Present Perfect: "I have finished."Past Perfect: He said he had finished.
Past Simple: "I went there."Past Perfect: She said she had gone there.
will: "I will call you."would: He said he would call me.
can: "I can help."could: She said she could help.
may: "It may rain."might: He said it might rain.
must: "You must leave."had to: She said I had to leave.
When NOT to backshift

1. Reporting verb is present tense: "She says she is tired." (not "was")

2. The information is still true / a permanent fact: "He told me Paris is the capital of France."

3. Modal verbs that don't change: would, could, should, might, ought to already express distance — they do NOT backshift further: "She said she would help." (not "would have helped")

Reporting verbs & patterns (B2)

Using varied reporting verbs makes your English much more advanced. Each verb has a pattern:

PatternVerbsExample
verb + thatsay, explain, claim, admit, deny, insist, agreeShe admitted that she had made a mistake.
verb + to + Vpromise, refuse, offer, agree, threatenHe promised to call back.
verb + sb + to + Vask, tell, warn, invite, advise, remind, encourageShe warned me not to be late.
verb + V-ingsuggest, recommend, deny, admitHe suggested going to the cinema.
