Comparing
Порівняння — comparatives, superlatives, and comparison structures at B2
Comparative adjectives — forms
+
Short (1 syl): -er than — fast → faster than, big → bigger than
+
Long (2+ syl): more/less … than — expensive → more expensive than
+
Irregular: good→better, bad→worse, far→further/farther, little→less, much→more
✎
Double consonant rule: big→bigger, hot→hotter, thin→thinner
Superlative adjectives — forms
+
Short: the + -est — fast → the fastest
+
Long: the most/least … — expensive → the most expensive
+
Irregular: good→the best, bad→the worst, far→the furthest, little→the least, much→the most
Key B2 comparison structures
StructureUseExample
as … asEqual comparison (same degree)She's as tall as her brother.
not as … asUnequal (less than)This is not as good as the first one.
much / far / a lot + comparativeLarge differenceThis is far more interesting than I expected.
a bit / slightly + comparativeSmall differenceHe's slightly taller than me.
the + comparative, the + comparativeParallel increase / proportionThe more you practise, the better you get.
comparative + and + comparativeProgressive changeIt's getting colder and colder.
by far the + superlativeEmphatic superlativeThis is by far the best film I've seen.
one of the + superlative + plural nounNear-superlativeShe's one of the smartest people I know.
like / asSimilarity (like = preposition, as = conjunction)He swims like a fish. / Do as I say.
whereas / whileContrast comparisonShe likes jazz, whereas he prefers classical.
Modifiers — making comparisons stronger or weaker
Stronger (large difference)
much / far / a lot / considerably + comparative
"This is much easier than I thought."
"She's far more experienced than him."
"It costs considerably less online."
Weaker (small difference)
a bit / slightly / a little / rather + comparative
"He's a bit taller than me."
"This is slightly more complex."
"It's a little colder today."
like vs as — common confusion
like — preposition (+ noun/pronoun)
He looks like his father. (= resembles)
She sings like a professional.
In spoken English: "Do it like I showed you." (informal)
as — conjunction (+ subject + verb)
Do it as I showed you. (= in the way that)
She worked as a nurse for 10 years. (= in the role of)
As I said, this is important.
⚠
Common mistake ✗ "She works like a teacher." (implies she's not really a teacher — just behaves like one) ✓ "She works as a teacher." (she IS a teacher — her job)
